Traveler, not a tourist.

Tern is a planning desk for people who would rather understand a place than tick it off. It keeps the map of where you have been, works out what that actually adds up to, and builds the next trip around it — distances, visas, airports and all.

Founded 2026· New Haven, Connecticut

Why a tern

The Arctic tern makes the longest migration of any animal alive. Every year it flies from the Arctic to the Antarctic and back — roughly 70,000 km, pole to pole, chasing the light so that it lives in near-perpetual summer. Over a life of thirty-odd years that comes to something like three return trips to the Moon.

It is not a bird that wanders. It goes a very long way, on purpose, and it knows exactly why it is going. That seemed like the right thing to name a travel app after.

Why I built it

I love travelling, and I have never found the planning part to match. The map of where you have been lives in one place, the visa rules in another, the flights somewhere else again, and none of them talk to each other.

I am an MBA student at Yale, and I built Tern to put those pieces on one desk — and to make the case that travelling well is a skill worth getting better at, not a scoreboard.

What the name is not

“Traveler, not a tourist” is a philosophy about how to go somewhere, not a verdict on anybody. Nothing here grades you. The numbers exist so you can see the shape of your own travelling — which corners of the world you keep returning to, and which you have never once looked at.

What it will not do

Tern holds no fares, no room rates and no flight schedules, and it will not invent them. Where a real number is needed it names what it actually knows — the airport, the distance, the visa rule, the arrivals figure and its year — and hands the rest to the people who hold live data, with the search already filled in.

Every figure on this site traces back to a published source. Where something is a judgement rather than a measurement, it says so.

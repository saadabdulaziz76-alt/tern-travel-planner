Traveler, not a tourist.
A planning desk that refuses to invent numbers.

Saad Alarifi · Yale SOM · AI Foundations · 26 Oct 2026

The problemPlanning is fragmented

The map of where you have been lives in one place. The visa rules in another. The flights somewhere else again.

Nothing answers the question that matters:

Given everywhere I have already been, where should I go next — and what will it take?

The constraintOne rule shaped everything

No number appears unless it traces to a published source.

Cheap to say. Expensive to keep. It deleted three features:

  • a "top 5% of travellers" badge — interpolated, indefensible
  • a price estimator — needs a paid API
  • flight schedules — same

A plausible invented number is worse than no number. A missing figure announces itself; a wrong one does not.

SafetyThree risks, three defences

  • Injection — 14 patterns, checked raw, normalised, squashed and base64-decoded. Blocked before the model: zero tokens.
  • Output to DOM — a real XSS found in review: model text into innerHTML at 17 sites, could have stolen a session token.
  • Runaway cost — daily, session and per-minute ceilings. The throttle fired in live testing.

LimitsSaid plainly

  • No prices, fares or schedules. It names the airports and hands off to sites that hold live data.
  • No external user test. Flagged 14 Oct, never closed. Everything in Impact is author testing and source validation.
  • UNESCO runs ~15% over official — stated on the page.
  • The verdict threshold is a guess — 40% was never tuned.

What I would tell youThree things

Measure before you architect. The two-tier design was a timing result, not an instinct.

Your tests are code too. They can be confidently wrong.

A rule that costs you features is the one doing work.
